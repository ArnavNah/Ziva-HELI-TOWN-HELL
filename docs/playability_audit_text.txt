<div class="eyebrow">Heli-Town Hell / measured playability review / 21 October 2026</div><h1>Why the game feels hard to control.</h1><p class="lead">There is a real aiming bug. Movement and camera momentum add delay, the whole town rebuilds on deployment, and XP can remain outside your collection range.</p>
<div class="grid"><div class="card"><div class="metric">~6 seconds</div><h3>Deployment</h3><p>142 buildings are generated at runtime. World preparation waits 215 frames.</p></div><div class="card"><div class="metric">18.5 metres</div><h3>Coasting</h3><p>Distance travelled in the first second after releasing forward thrust.</p></div><div class="card"><div class="metric">65 metres</div><h3>Cursor mismatch</h3><p>The gun stayed locked while the cursor moved this far away on the ground.</p></div></div>
<section><h2>What to fix first</h2><ol><li>Release sticky enemy targeting and show the real reachable aim point.</li><li>Reduce unwanted braking/camera delay after choosing the intended control style.</li><li>Fix loot altitude and align XP accessibility with combat distance.</li><li>Cache world geometry and give generation a real time budget and progress counter.</li><li>Verify a full combat run with movement, upgrades and retry.</li></ol><p class="muted">This is a diagnosis. Game code and the player's save were not changed.</p></section>
<section><div class="eyebrow">1 · Confirmed bug · fix first</div><h2>The gun can stay locked after you move the mouse away</h2><p>Hovering an enemy sets its target score to 100. The automatic selection then requires a replacement to beat 120, although its normal scores are much smaller. The current target is not cleared when it leaves range. The timer called settle_timer is decremented but never used to release the lock.</p><p class="note"><strong>Evidence:</strong> In the probe, moving the cursor to empty ground 65 m away from the selected target did not release it. Moving that enemy beyond 140 m still did not release it.</p><p><strong>Repair:</strong> Separate direct mouse intent from optional aim assistance. Release/revalidate the target on cursor exit, range, visibility and death; compare fresh scores on the same scale. Add tests for leaving the cursor, switching targets, walls and targets leaving range.</p><small><code>src/player/player_turret.gd:79</code></small></section><section><div class="eyebrow">2 · Confirmed aiming mismatch</div><h2>The crosshair shows the mouse, even when the barrel cannot point there</h2><p>The HUD reticle is attached directly to the mouse. The gun is independently limited to ±110° yaw and −75°/+25° pitch. A rear cursor position is therefore unreachable, but the HUD gives no indication. Mouse projection also uses the y=0 plane rather than the actual building/aircraft surface.</p><p class="note"><strong>Evidence:</strong> After allowing the turret to settle with a cursor behind the aircraft, barrel direction was about 66.7° away from the requested direction. This is a clamp/feedback mismatch, not proof that all yaw math is reversed.</p><p><strong>Repair:</strong> Raycast the visible cursor into the actual world with a fallback plane. Show the real gun/impact aim point or a clear out-of-arc cue. Keep camera update order, launch direction and reticle based on the same aiming solution. Decide explicitly whether rear aiming rotates the aircraft or stays restricted.</p><small><code>src/ui/hud.gd:233</code></small></section><section><div class="eyebrow">3 · Confirmed startup cost</div><h2>The whole town is rebuilt before every deployment</h2><p>The scene requests a 16×16 grid. Each run creates procedural roads, building meshes, collision shapes, props and field dressing at runtime. DetailMesh builds packed vertex/index arrays on the main thread. There is no baked-map or reusable building-mesh cache in this construction path.</p><p class="note"><strong>Evidence:</strong> The measured town contains 142 buildings, 713 MeshInstance3D nodes and 2,219 descendant nodes. Two diagnostic runs loaded in 6.11 s and 5.97 s. The latest world-generation phase had a 302.6 ms maximum frame interval; the largest interval across the whole deployment was 763.3 ms.</p><p><strong>Repair:</strong> Cache/prebuild repeated intact archetype meshes and collision resources, or save a generated town as a scene/resource. Share instances where possible. Build required near-spawn content before revealing gameplay and schedule the remaining work only if a real streaming system is designed. Avoid rebuilding identical geometry on retry.</p><small><code>src/world/city_generator.gd:38</code></small></section><section><div class="eyebrow">4 · Confirmed loading behavior</div><h2>World generation has a frame-count delay and misleading progress</h2><p>Generation yields by loop count: roughly 85 road-marking yields, 64 block yields and 64 field-dressing yields for this grid. That is about 213 frame waits before readiness, regardless of how little work a batch contains. The loading UI independently pushes world progress toward 95% after 0.8 s, rather than measuring completed batches.</p><p class="note"><strong>Evidence:</strong> The actual loader spent 215 frames in WORLD preparation in both runs. At a 60 FPS cap, 213 frame waits alone represent roughly 3.55 s. At lower frame rates, the same construction schedule takes longer.</p><p><strong>Repair:</strong> Use a measured millisecond work budget per frame instead of unconditional fixed-count yields. Publish completed/total generation work to the loader or show an honest indeterminate preparation state. Preserve the existing readiness contract and do not reveal an unfinished collision world.</p><small><code>src/world/city_generator.gd:67</code></small></section><section><div class="eyebrow">5 · Source-confirmed distinction</div><h2>Buildings appearing late in flight can be visibility cutoff, not loading</h2><p>Building meshes are hidden beyond 155 m with a 15 m transition margin. Several props and vegetation batches have similar 155–160 m cutoffs. The city is generated before session activation, so this path does not load buildings one by one as you fly.</p><p class="note"><strong>Evidence:</strong> The source applies a visibility range to the building mesh and main_game.gd waits for generation_complete before setting is_prepared. A moving-camera pop-in capture was not performed, so this explains a possible appearance symptom rather than proving every instance of it.</p><p><strong>Repair:</strong> Distinguish startup time from distance pop-in when reproducing the complaint. Align visibility distances, inexpensive distant silhouettes/LOD and fog with the normal camera. Do not simply disable all culling; the city contributes measurable rendering cost.</p><small><code>src/world/destructible_building.gd:56</code></small></section><section><div class="eyebrow">6 · Measured feel problem · design choice</div><h2>Flight and camera are tuned for substantial momentum</h2><p>Releasing input applies a gentle glide drag. W/S are heading-relative forward/reverse, A/D rotate, and Q/E strafe. The chase camera has separate yaw and position lag, roll, idle float, changing FOV and collision zoom. These layers make the aircraft feel less direct than a typical WASD survivor-game character.</p><p class="note"><strong>Evidence:</strong> The baseline helicopter reached about 36 m/s. After releasing thrust it travelled another 18.47 m during the first second and still moved at 9.34 m/s. During a sustained turn, camera heading lagged about 12.58°. These are separate controlled empty-world measurements.</p><p><strong>Repair:</strong> Fix aiming first, then tune a clear movement contract: stronger optional auto-braking, less chase-camera lag and less roll/float for combat. Verify collision zoom in town. If camera-relative WASD is desired, change the movement/input/UI contract deliberately rather than silently swapping keys. The current acceleration math is not itself proven broken.</p><small><code>src/player/player_helicopter.gd:641</code></small></section><section><div class="eyebrow">7 · Measured collection and pacing issue</div><h2>Nearby XP travels quickly, but the combat and collection ranges disagree</h2><p>XP attraction uses a 48 m radius with a reduced vertical-distance penalty. Combat assistance can select an enemy up to 140 m away. Distant kills can leave gems outside attraction range, requiring a trip to collect them. LootManager also explicitly halves listed enemy XP, rounds down and clamps it; early small enemies commonly drop only 1 XP.</p><p class="note"><strong>Evidence:</strong> With the helicopter at 14 m altitude, stationary collection took about 0.32 s from 20 m, 0.50 s from 40 m and 0.55 s from 47 m horizontal distance. Gems at 48 and 60 m did not begin attracting within the 5 s test. At 48 m the remaining vertical penalty places the gem just outside the radius. The first level needs 10 cumulative XP; the next needs 25.</p><p><strong>Repair:</strong> Do not blindly increase suction speed again. Align credited-kill/engagement range with a clearly defined XP attraction policy and an altitude-aware collection rule. Decide target time/kills per level, then tune the drop multiplier and threshold curve together. XP transfer on arrival already works and must remain once-only.</p><small><code>src/loot/pickup.gd:123</code></small></section><section><div class="eyebrow">8 · Confirmed placement bug</div><h2>Airborne loot snaps down because its idle height is captured too early</h2><p>The loot manager adds a pickup to the scene tree before assigning its spawn position. _ready immediately stores initial_base_y from the default position. The next idle animation resets position.y around that old base rather than the chosen drop altitude.</p><p class="note"><strong>Evidence:</strong> A real loot-manager drop at y=25.5 moved to approximately y=0.03 on its first idle update. This can make air loot appear to vanish or move away from where an aircraft was killed.</p><p><strong>Repair:</strong> Assign the intended local/global spawn transform before the pickup captures its bobbing baseline, or provide an explicit initialization method that sets both consistently. If ground drops are desired, project them to a real surface instead of accidentally snapping to y=0.</p><small><code>src/loot/loot_manager.gd:165</code></small></section>
<section><h2>Does low FPS explain everything?</h2><p>No constant low-FPS problem was established by these short samples. The city and shadows cost frame time, and the slower combat frames exceeded a 60 FPS budget. The aiming and inertia problems were also reproduced in a small isolated world.</p><div class="tablewrap"><table><thead><tr><th>Configuration</th><th>Median</th><th>p95</th><th>Draw calls</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Idle town</td><td>7.50 ms</td><td>13.40 ms</td><td>209</td></tr><tr><td>Same scene, city hidden</td><td>3.23 ms</td><td>7.88 ms</td><td>70</td></tr><tr><td>Town, sun shadows off</td><td>4.92 ms</td><td>8.34 ms</td><td>157</td></tr><tr><td>Separate 24-enemy combat sample</td><td>11.67 ms</td><td>17.30 ms</td><td>557</td></tr></tbody></table></div><p class="muted">p95 is the frame time at the slower end of the sample. 60 FPS allows about 16.67 ms per frame. <section><h2>What was tested, and what remains uncertain</h2><ul><li>Source inspection plus isolated Godot probes; no production gameplay source, control bindings, balance values or player save was changed.</li><li>Godot 4.7.1, Compatibility/OpenGL 3.3, Intel Iris Xe, 1280×720. Profiling disabled FPS/VSync caps only inside the isolated process. It did not change the user settings.</li><li>Frame samples are short (150 measured frames after 40 warm-up frames), from a fixed initial camera and a separate controlled 24-enemy combat sample. Twenty-one enemy nodes remained at the end. These are diagnostics, not a long-session performance guarantee or a hand-played match.</li><li>The two initial profiling runs overlapped briefly with a small headless controls probe. Both had the same 215-frame world preparation count; timing is approximate and machine-specific.</li><li>The rendered profiling process completed and wrote its measurements, then emitted renderer/resource-cleanup errors at exit; the runner therefore returned nonzero. Those errors are recorded, not treated as a clean test pass. They do not establish a particular in-game memory leak or explain the aiming bug.</li><li>The focused controls/pickups probe completed without script errors, with ObjectDB cleanup warnings on the last run. The existing XP pacing suite passed 6/6.</li><li>No moving-camera building pop-in recording, long soak test, controller hardware test, or continuous combat playthrough was performed. Mouse/aim and collection results were driven deterministically through the real methods.</li></ul><p><a href="PLAYABILITY_AUDIT_2026-10-21.md">Detailed source review</a> · <a href="../artifacts/playability-audit-20261021/controls-pickups.json">Controls and pickup measurements</a> · <a href="../artifacts/playability-audit-20261021/world-profile.json">World measurements</a></p></section>
