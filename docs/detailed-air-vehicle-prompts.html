Shared style prompt · included in complete prompts
Create one original low-poly enemy aircraft for a colorful arcade helicopter
combat game. Use the same chunky, playful, exaggerated military art direction
as the ground vehicle collection, inspired by Megabonk.

ART DIRECTION:
Broad flat surfaces, visible polygon facets, squat fuselages, thick wings,
oversized functional equipment, and bold silhouettes. Slightly toy-like,
threatening, and a little absurd. Every unit must be recognizable from an
elevated helicopter camera through its shape before its color.

GEOMETRY:
Genuinely low-poly geometry with flat shading. Use approximately 6-10 sides
for small cylinders. Bevel only silhouette-defining edges. Rotor blades,
wings, fins, and mounts have visible thickness. Avoid small fragile details.
Separate moving machinery from the airframe for later animation.

MATERIALS:
Matte painted armor, charcoal mechanics, dark opaque canopy panels, and a
few broad accent panels. Minimal texture noise. Large painted vents and
simple panel divisions. Match the approved ground assets in shading and
surface detail. Colored lenses should remain legible without bloom.

PRESENTATION:
One aircraft only, fully assembled in a neutral flight pose, centered on a
plain light-gray background. Elevated front three-quarter view showing the
nose, roof, one side, wings, and propulsion. Soft neutral studio lighting.
Leave generous space around every rotor, wingtip, tail, and weapon.
Show stationary physical rotor blades, never motion-blurred discs.
No scenery, ground plane, stand, cast-shadow platform, text, labels, or effects.
Negative prompt · included in complete prompts
Photorealistic, high-poly, subdivision surfaces, dense bevels, realistic
military simulation, intricate machinery, individual bolts, noisy weathering,
heavy scratches, rust, chrome, glossy plastic, transparent cockpit glass,
visible pilot, crew, tiny markings, text, logos, watermark, thin antennas,
razor-thin wings, paper-thin blades, fused rotors, warped symmetry,
extra propellers, missing blades, blocked gun muzzle, detached weapons,
exploded view, multiple aircraft, cropped wingtip, cropped rotor, display
stand, diorama, background scenery, motion blur, spinning translucent disc,
smoke, fire, engine trails, muzzle flashes, falling bombs, loose mines,
energy beams, shield bubble, sparks, floating holograms.
3D delivery requirements · optional when copying
Deliver a modular game asset. Join static armor details into the relevant
parent mesh. Keep Airframe, each Rotor assembly, aiming Gun/Turret, and
any moving BayDoors or Projector mechanically separate where applicable.
Do not create a separate object for every panel, bolt, vent, or blade.

ROTORS AND FANS:
Each rotor assembly includes its hub and blades with a pivot at the true
shaft center. All blades of that rotor rotate together. Keep fixed fan ducts
separate. Leave real blade-to-duct and blade-to-airframe clearance.
Helicopter main rotors spin about their local shaft, tail rotors about their
side-facing shaft. Keep fixed fan ducts and wings stationary while their rotor assemblies spin.
No baked blur discs. Reuse compatible mesh/material resources across repeated
propulsion units; keep individual placement and animation transforms.

WEAPONS AND SOCKETS:
Provide named Muzzle markers at the visible barrel openings and launch or
drop sockets for each functional launcher, bay, or dispenser as applicable.
Muzzle local -Z points outward along the actual barrel. Separate aiming
cradles from static mounts. Keep gun traverse and doors clear of the hull.
Model only the equipment required by the unit's role; support drones do not
need guns. Additional decorative equipment does not imply new gameplay.

EXPORT:
Export GLB/glTF 2.0 with sensible object names, applied scale, clean normals,
and no accidental interior fragments. Final Godot orientation: +Y up,
-Z forward. Use meters; root at a documented central airframe reference.
Keep rotors and sockets aligned in the assembled bind pose.

Suggested complete-asset budgets, including blades and weapons:
Small drones: approximately 800-2,000 triangles.
Regular aircraft: approximately 2,000-4,000 triangles.
Heavy bomber/VTOL: approximately 3,000-5,000 triangles.
These are design targets, not measured performance guarantees.

Prefer one shared color atlas/material for painted surfaces and mechanics;
use an optional second emissive material for lenses or projector lenses.
No baked lighting, transparent shield mesh, trails, or particle effects.
Keep original source files and store new enemy assets under assets/Enemy/.

Generation does not guarantee usable topology, separation, UVs, pivots,
triangle counts, or sockets. Inspect and correct those in the modeling stage.
Match the actual gameplay scale and camera before finalizing import.
This is an art asset specification; preserve existing AI, weapon balance,
projectile behavior, and collision dimensions during later integration.
01

Attack Gunship

attack_gunship · Helicopter · Chin autocannon
Create a low-poly military attack helicopter focused on an articulated
chin autocannon.

SILHOUETTE:
A narrow chisel nose, stepped tandem cockpit, chunky central engine body,
long tapered tail boom, short weapon wings, and one broad four-blade main
rotor. Add a small side-facing tail rotor. Keep the cockpit and tail
unmistakably helicopter-shaped from above.

BODY AND COLOR:
Dark olive armor, charcoal engine vents, a dark red opaque canopy, and
small mustard-yellow recognition panels. Use two simple engine housings
behind the cockpit and short thick landing skids. Armor is broad and
faceted, with only a few large panel divisions.

WEAPON:
One prominent chin autocannon with a thick receiver and faceted barrel.
Show a distinct yaw mount and elevation cradle with clearance below the nose.
Small compact side stores may remain secondary to the gun.

PROPULSION AND PARTS:
Separate main rotor, tail rotor, airframe, turret shell, and aiming gun.
Pose every blade stationary and keep the complete rotor span in frame.

CHARACTER:
An aggressive nose-forward attack helicopter with an oversized chin gun.
Keep it slim, with the chin cannon as its defining weapon.
No giant cargo cabin, additional main rotor, or weapons firing.
Slim armored nose + single main rotor
02

Interceptor

interceptor · Fixed-wing jet · High-speed burst fire
Create a low-poly high-speed interceptor jet with a long aggressive profile.

SILHOUETTE:
A long faceted spear-like nose, narrow central fuselage, sharply swept
triangular delta wings, one tall swept tail fin, and one oversized rear
exhaust. Keep the whole shape long and streamlined, with a clearly readable
arrowhead planform. Wing tips should be thick enough to survive
low-poly conversion without becoming paper-thin.

BODY AND COLOR:
Desaturated steel-blue armor, charcoal wing undersides and exhaust,
small warm ivory panels, and a brick-red dorsal stripe. Dark opaque canopy
set behind the long nose. Minimal panel divisions.

WEAPON:
Two short reinforced forward gun openings near the front wing roots.
The guns communicate rapid precision bursts without creating a huge
exposed turret. Keep the nose clean and straight.

PROPULSION AND PARTS:
One dark rear jet outlet, simple wing-root intakes, and retracted landing
gear. No rotors, propellers, articulated wings, or unnecessary mechanisms.
Provide clear forward weapon sockets for later integration.

CHARACTER:
A flying dart: pointed, fast, and uncompromising.
No broad bomber fuselage, twin tails, heavy external payload, or effects.
Long spear nose + tight delta silhouette
03

Heavy Bomber

heavy_bomber · Fixed-wing jet · Heavy payload
Create a low-poly heavy tactical bomber with exaggerated carrying capacity.

SILHOUETTE:
A thick slab-like fuselage, blunt armored cockpit, very broad mildly swept
wings, two large underwing engine nacelles, a substantial horizontal tail,
and one tall central tail fin. It should feel broad, weighty, and slower
than every regular fighter in the collection.

BODY AND COLOR:
Muted olive wings, blue-gray fuselage armor, charcoal engines, and large
sand-yellow recognition panels. Use simple dark cockpit windows and a
few broad roof vents. Avoid intricate panel seams.

PAYLOAD:
A prominent central belly bomb bay with two closed rectangular doors,
plus compact closed equipment housings under the outer wings.
Give the underside enough thickness for the bay to be structurally plausible.
Do not hang dozens of separate bombs from the wings.

PARTS:
Separate bomb-bay doors around actual hinge lines if opening is required
later. Show the closed-door bind pose in the reference. Keep engine nacelles
static and provide a central drop socket below the bay.

CHARACTER:
A chunky flying cargo box adapted for bombing. Its broad planform should
remain clear beside the slim fighters.
No four-engine redesign, extra turrets, falling bombs, or open exploded bay.
Broad wings + thick fuselage + two engines
04

Torpedo VTOL

torpedo_vtol · Ducted-fan VTOL · Twin guided projectiles
Create a low-poly heavy VTOL attack aircraft with two large ducted lift fans
and two long guided-projectile launch housings.

SILHOUETTE:
A chunky rectangular armored fuselage, short broad cross-wing, and one
large circular fan pod near each wingtip. Each fan has a thick polygonal
protective ring with visible stationary blades inside. Two long belly
launcher housings project forward beneath the body.

BODY AND COLOR:
Muted olive and sand armor, charcoal fan recesses, brick-red launcher
housings, and pale cyan opaque cockpit panels. Use a blunt sloped nose
and a compact rear stabilizer.

WEAPON:
Exactly two substantial elongated launch housings, one per side beneath
the wing roots. Use dark recessed front openings and thick retaining mounts.
The torpedo-like payload is an aerial guided projectile; do not turn the
vehicle into a boat or add naval equipment.

PROPULSION AND PARTS:
Separate the two fan rotors from the fixed ducts and airframe.
Keep blade tips clear of the rings. A small nose sensor is sufficient;
the belly launchers should dominate the weapon silhouette.

CHARACTER:
A heavy hovering delivery platform. Its defining features are the circular
fan ducts and bulky launcher pair, distinct from the gun helicopter.
No open tiltrotors, nautical details, missile trails, or firing effects.
Twin circular fan pods + long belly launchers
05

Kamikaze Drone

kamikaze_drone · Quadrotor · Diving explosive attack
Create a low-poly kamikaze quadrotor with an oversized armored explosive core.

SILHOUETTE:
A compact four-arm X frame surrounding a thick faceted central warhead body.
Exactly four small two-blade rotors at the arm ends. The central core should
be large and blunt relative to the short rotor arms.
Give the front a short downward-angled impact wedge for a diving attitude.

BODY AND COLOR:
Brick-red warhead armor, charcoal arms and propellers, olive rear casing,
and small yellow caution panels without text. Use a dark red inset center
that can receive a subtle runtime warning glow later.
The model must still read clearly with emission disabled.

EQUIPMENT:
No separate gun, launcher, bomb hanging below, or attack turret.
The entire vehicle is the threat. Use a broad front impact plate and a few
simple reinforcement ribs to communicate the heavy central payload.
Avoid intricate explosive wiring or tiny components.

PARTS:
Separate all four rotor assemblies and keep blade clearances intact.
The warhead shell remains one static body; any warning light material should
be isolated from the painted armor.

CHARACTER:
A compact angry flying charge with an unmistakable red center.
Make the core dominate its outline so it reads as a flying charge.
No plane wings, nose propeller, explosion, smoke, exposed wiring, or loose bombs.
Compact X frame + oversized red warhead core
06

Shield Drone

shield_drone · Support quadrotor · Protects nearby enemies
Create a low-poly shield-support quadrotor with a large physical shield
projector built into its armored body.

SILHOUETTE:
A broad flattened hexagonal center, four thick short arms, and four compact
rotor assemblies inside sturdy protective ducts. Keep the duct rings simple
and polygonal. The overall form is broad and defensive rather than pointed.

BODY AND COLOR:
Muted olive-blue armor, charcoal fan recesses, sand recognition panels,
and one large pale blue projector lens. Use thick layered upper armor plates
with very few panel lines. The blue hardware must be visible from above
and from the front three-quarter camera.

SUPPORT EQUIPMENT:
A substantial central projector housing with a recessed faceted blue lens
on the upper-front face and a smaller underside emitter.
These are physical mounted components. Do not model the protective bubble,
floating rings, or holographic shield symbols.
This support unit carries no gun or missile launcher.

PARTS:
Separate four fan rotors from their fixed ducts.
Keep projector lens material separately controllable for a runtime pulse.
Use solid lens geometry; transparency is unnecessary.

CHARACTER:
A little flying armored guardian. Its broad shell and blue projector should
identify it without relying on an active shield effect.
No giant energy sphere, repair tools, attack barrel, or transparent armor.
Broad hexagonal shell + blue projector lens
